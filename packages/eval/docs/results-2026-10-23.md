# Results, 2026-10-23

Measured with `@gitgoblin/eval` on the dates given; every table can be
regenerated from the files under `data/` with the commands in the README.
Read `design.md` for what each experiment does and `research-2026-10-23.md`
for why.

## E2. Intel opportunities against later changes (calibration set)

Seventeen repositories (the calibration and held-out sets from
`packages/intel/docs/calibration-2026-10-22.md`) analyzed at their
default-branch commit as of 2026-04-25, with the clock set to that date and
link and dependency checks off; every file-anchored finding graded against
HEAD on 2026-10-23.

Of 298 opportunities, 274 were issue, feature request, stale pull request,
triage or CI kinds (read from the live API, so not gradeable this way; the
issue classifier has its own backtest in intel). The 24 file-anchored
findings:

| kind | findings | acted upon | by an outsider | by a maintainer | control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| test_gap (skipped tests) | 7 | 3 (43%) | 0 | 3 | 0% |
| tech_debt (old TODOs) | 15 | 1 (7%) | 1 | 0 | 20% |
| broken_link | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0% |
| deprecation | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0% |
| all | 24 | 5 (21%) | 2 (8%) | 3 | 13% |

"Acted upon" means the excerpt was removed by a commit touching at most 50
files. "Control" is the share of seeded random lines from the same files at
T that were gone at HEAD, the background churn an acted-upon rate has to
beat (an idea borrowed from intel's own `scripts/backtest-code.ts`). By rank
in the report: top five, 3 of 5 acted upon against 0% control; ranks 6 to
15, 2 of 6; below 15, 0 of 13 against 23% control. The ordering carries
signal even at this size, and old TODOs were touched less than the lines
around them.

Baseline in the same repositories: of 76 issues carrying a starter label at
T, 7 (9%) were resolved by an outsider's pull request in the same window, 6
by a maintainer's, 4 closed without one, and 59 (78%) were still open.

**Hand-read calibration of the automatic label.** Every "changed" finding
was read against the commit that changed it. Before the grader learned to
spot sweeps and moved excerpts, 4 of 8 "changed" items were incidental: a
330-file ESM migration (mocha), a 741-file refactor that re-wrapped a
comment (ruff), a Karma-to-Playwright switch that deleted the file (mocha),
and a deprecation rename that touched a skipped test's assertion (guzzle).
With the sweep threshold and the whole-repository search for moved
excerpts, 4 of the 5 counted items are real (jq's lexer TODO resolved by an
outsider, vite's skipped test removed, Pillow's xfail narrowed, ruff's
links fixed by an outsider) and 1 is incidental (guzzle). Treat "acted upon"
rates as about 80% precise.

What this says: in polished projects, the work gitgoblin finds outside the
tracker is scarce (24 items across 17 repositories) and old TODOs are mostly
left alone, but skipped tests get attention, and the top of the ranking is
where the action was. The pool-based run below is the fairer test, because
discovery surfaces rougher repositories than these.

**Second sample, graded the same way by intel's own script.** The intel
session ran `packages/intel/scripts/backtest-code.ts` on a disjoint set of 19
repositories (sqlalchemy, httpie, aiohttp, pip-tools, pip, mitmproxy, sphinx,
jinja, werkzeug, undici, fastify, koa, got, gorilla/mux, urfave/cli,
BurntSushi/toml, serde, clap, caddy) at their 2025-10-23 commit with a
365-day window, after adopting the same "targeted" criterion (first post-T
commit in which the excerpt is absent, at most 50 files touched):

| kind | findings | gone at HEAD | targeted | control (targeted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ci_rot | 30 | 43% | 40% | 5% |
| broken_link | 16 | 63% | 31% | 5% |
| tech_debt | 24 | 33% | 8% | 5% |
| test_gap (untested files) | 9 | 25% | 0% | 5% |
| deprecation | 5 | 0% | 0% | 5% |
| docs_drift | 5 | 0% | 0% | 5% |

Both samples agree: CI rot and broken links are what maintainers fix
deliberately; old TODOs mostly vanish inside refactors (2 of 8 removals
targeted there, 1 of 15 here); untested-file and deprecation findings were
not acted upon in either sample. The five surviving deprecation findings
were deliberate `datetime.utcnow()` calls under `examples/` and a docs
helper, and intel now treats merely deprecated calls under examples, docs,
scripts and benchmarks like those in tests. Changes made on this evidence
in intel: untested-file value prior 0.45 to 0.35; vendored directory names
(`_vendor`, `3rdparty`, `extern`), stubs, deferred and compatibility imports
no longer produce deprecation findings.

**Issue-backed opportunities.** The same run accepted 245 issue-backed
opportunities across 14 repositories. Because intel reads live issue data,
their fate can only be judged later; `intel issues` records it against the
analysis time and can be re-run in months. One thing is visible now: 1 of
the 245 was open with a closing pull request that had already merged (psf/
requests #4664, merged in 2023, ranked first for that repository), and
intel's "already fixed?" check did not flag it. Drift is rare in polished
projects, and it reaches the top rank when it happens.

## A pitfall worth recording

The first pool for E1 used `pushed:2026-03-22..2026-06-20` to pin the
candidates to T. GitHub's `pushed` qualifier filters on a repository's
*last* push, so that pool was exactly the set of repositories that stopped
being pushed after T: 478 repositories, 4 newcomer merges in four months
against 1,272 in the year before. The corrected pool uses star bands,
`created:<T-90d` and the open-ended `pushed:>T-180d`, which discovery's
own 180-day rule already implies for any repository eligible at T.
