# Landscape and literature, 2026-10-23

What exists for finding open-source work, what research says predicts
success, and what that implies for gitgoblin. Written to decide what to
measure and what to build; `design.md` is the result.

## Tools people use today

| Tool | How it ranks | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub Explore "Contribute" and personalized recommendations | Repositories you starred or contributed to, open `good first issue` and `help wanted` issues | Anything unlabelled; whether labelled issues get resolved |
| GitHub search, `gh search issues --label "good first issue"` | Label, language, recency | Same |
| Copilot Chat (documented workaround) | Asks for repositories with starter labels and over 100 stars | Same, plus a star floor |
| firstissue.dev, goodfirstissue.dev, up-for-grabs.net, CodeTriage | Curated or label-driven lists; CodeTriage mails one open issue a day from a project you pick | Same; curation lags |
| OSS Insight, ecosyste.ms, deps.dev, OpenSSF Scorecard and criticality score | Activity, dependents, security posture | Built for analysts and funders, not for "where should I spend a weekend" |

Every contributor-facing tool is label-based and most add a popularity floor.
None reports whether outsiders' work in a project actually gets merged, and
none looks for work that has no issue.

## What the research says

- **Labels are a weak proxy.** RecGFI (ICSE 2022) trains on "resolved by a
  newcomer" rather than on the label because labelled issues are "sparse and
  sometimes unsuitable"; it reaches 0.85 AUC with content, background and
  dynamics features, and cross-project transfer costs 7 to 16 points of AUC.
  GFI-Bot (FSE 2022) deploys it. PFIRec (ASE 2023) personalizes on the
  newcomer's expertise and halves failed attempts. Two 2024-2025 studies
  (a METU thesis; LabelMate, arXiv 2609.04055) find existing labels disagree
  with issue content often enough that labels are poor ground truth.
- **Newcomer success on labelled issues is falling.** A 2021-2025
  longitudinal study of 37 popular projects (arXiv 2604.27532: 406,826
  issues, 1,117 newcomer pull requests on labelled issues) finds the share of
  labelled issues declining from 2024 and the merge rate of newcomer pull
  requests on them falling from 61.9% to 42.2%; pull request size and
  description length did not predict the outcome.
- **What predicts merged work.** Across 470,925 npm pull requests (Dey et
  al. 2020, AUC 0.94) the strongest predictors are the project's own
  pull-request volume and acceptance rate and review-phase signals; a 2024
  follow-up (arXiv 2410.14695, 1.8M pull requests) finds ecosystem experience
  helps newcomers most and that newcomer outcomes are the hardest to
  predict. For automated agents, issues that led to merged pull requests
  were short, well scoped and pointed at the relevant artifacts; issues
  needing external setup merged less (arXiv 2512.21426, AUC 0.72).
- **Mentoring and responsiveness matter more than task choice.** Studies of
  starter issues (Tan et al.; arXiv 2302.05058) find expert involvement is
  what gets a newcomer's issue solved.

## Implications

1. **Welcomeness must be measured from behaviour.** The literature's
   strongest predictor of a merged pull request is the project's own
   acceptance behaviour. Discovery's `newcomerFriendliness` today is files
   and labels; intel's welcomeness already uses external merge rate and
   response times. The backtest in `design.md` checks which of these
   predicts newcomer outcomes, and whether a direct "newcomer merge rate"
   signal would.
2. **Issues are not the whole of the work.** How much merged outside work
   references an issue, and how much of the tracker outsiders resolve, are
   measurable facts (E3). They decide how much weight to put on finding
   non-issue work.
3. **Ground truth is what happened next.** Audits by the author and model
   judgements are both opinions. Resolution by a newcomer (issues), merge of
   a newcomer's pull request (repositories), and a later fix of a finding
   (opportunities) are not.
4. **Personalization is the next step, not this one.** PFIRec's gains come
   from matching issues to the person. gitgoblin's goals and filters are a
   coarse version; a profile of the user's own merged work would be the
   fine one, once the generic ranking is shown to be sound.
